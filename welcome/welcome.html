A sample to read

For readers with dyslexia, a wall of tightly packed text in a thin typeface can feel like an obstacle course. Letters mirror and swap, lines blur together, and by the time the eye reaches the end of a long line, finding the start of the next one is a small act of navigation.

Small changes make outsized differences. Wider letter spacing keeps characters from crowding. Taller line height gives each line room to breathe. Bolding the first part of every word gives the eye an anchor, so reading becomes a glide from anchor to anchor. A warm background softens the glare that makes white screens tiring, and underlined links are easier to spot than colored ones.

Three things to know

  1. Pin the icon. Click the puzzle piece at the top right of Chrome and pin DyslexAid, so the popup is one click away on any page.
  2. Everything lives in the popup. Presets, switches, text size, and read aloud. Hover over anything there and it explains itself.
  3. Shortcuts work anywhere. Alt+Shift+R for the ruler, Alt+Shift+B for bold word starts, Alt+Shift+S to read aloud. With the ruler on, Alt+Up and Alt+Down move it without a mouse.
